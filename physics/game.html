Mobile controls & safety

Turn the device to look left/right; tilt it to look up/down. Hold the arrows to walk or steer; drag still works. Recenter aligns the device with your current view; the horizon stays level. Enable only when comfortable, seated or standing still in a clear space. Pause, Stop looking, or Exit view is always available by touch. Device look senses rotation only; it neither opens a camera nor estimates position from acceleration. For real displacement, separately set up Camera walking with the stationary marker below. Orientation readings are not saved or sent.

Set up real movement · printable marker

Put one marker upright on a stationary wall, or display it on another stationary screen. Measure the outer black square, then enter its width below. Keep the whole marker in the rear-camera preview, about 0.4–1.5 m away. Hold still until Tracking appears. Moving closer/farther or sideways then moves your in-game position; Device look separately controls rotation. This is approximate, marker-referenced room-scale motion—not marker-free AR or precision measurement. Use touch arrows for long journeys and raft steering.

    What is calculated, what is imagined?

    Raft draft follows Archimedes’ law and includes the 80 kg player. Two 0.25 m³ timber modules total 405 kg loaded, giving 81% immersion. One floats at 97%, but exceeds the mission’s 90% safety limit—not a physical sinking threshold. Waves, steering drag and terrain are illustrative. Capsules follow gravity (9.81 m/s²) from an 11 m/s throw, without auto-aim.

    The enlarged 10 g relay sample uses fixed-pressure calorimetry (no heat losses or container heat capacity). Melting its sensor triggers fictional machinery; it does not melt a whole bridge. Lab molecular motion, distances and atom colors are schematics, not measured trajectories or molecular dynamics. The scale lens is fictional; water vapor is invisible, unlike condensed droplets. New geometry and audio are generated by original local code—not borrowed game/media assets.
