Relativity

Special relativity reorganizes space and time so every inertial observer measures the same light speed. General relativity goes further: freely falling bodies follow spacetime geometry, and matter-energy helps determine that geometry.

Measurements change between observers; physical law does not.

Relativity does not say every statement is equally true. It gives exact transformation rules between reference frames and identifies invariants—quantities every observer can agree on after accounting for those rules.

c = constant

Invariant light speed

Every inertial observer measures the same vacuum light speed c, regardless of the source’s motion. Space and time coordinates must transform together to make that possible.

Δτ ≤ Δt

Proper time

A clock measures time along its own path through spacetime. Different paths between reunion events can accumulate different elapsed times; experiments verify this routinely.

free fall = geodesic

Equivalence & curvature

Locally, free fall removes the felt weight of gravity. Globally, tidal effects reveal curvature: neighboring free-fall paths converge or diverge.

A prediction becomes physics when nature can answer.

During the 1919 total solar eclipse, expeditions measured star positions near the darkened Sun and compared them with reference plates. The result supported general relativity’s prediction that gravity deflects light, although later measurements became much more precise.

Relativity is exact in scope—and incomplete as a final theory.

Relativity supplies stricter structure than Newtonian mechanics, but the classical geometry of general relativity has not yet been successfully unified with quantum mechanics in every regime.

The Newtonian limit

At low speeds and in weak, slowly changing gravitational fields, relativistic equations reduce to Newtonian mechanics and gravity with extremely high accuracy.

Quantum gravity

Near Planck-scale conditions or the deepest black-hole questions, treating spacetime as a smooth classical geometry is expected to fail. No experimentally confirmed complete quantum-gravity theory yet exists.

Not faster-than-light messaging

Relativity of simultaneity and quantum entanglement do not provide controllable signals faster than light. Causal influence remains constrained by light cones.

Relativity connects the smallest clocks to the whole universe.

GPS corrects relativistic clock rates. Particle accelerators require relativistic momentum. Cosmology uses general relativity to model an expanding universe. Gravitational-wave detectors measure spacetime disturbances smaller than a proton over kilometer scales.
