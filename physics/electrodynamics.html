Light & signals

Maxwell's equations predict waves that travel at one particular speed, and that prediction became the foundation of relativity. But light in matter is slower, wave packets have several distinct speeds, and a signal crossing the universe arrives changed. Telling these apart is most of the subject.

The speed was calculated before it was measured.

Maxwell combined the laws of electricity and magnetism and found that a changing electric field creates a magnetic field, whose change recreates the electric field. That loop can propagate on its own, at a speed built from two constants measured in tabletop experiments about charges and currents.

c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)

A speed from statics

Neither constant has anything obviously to do with light: one describes the force between charges, the other the force between currents. Their combination matched the measured speed of light, which is why light was identified as an electromagnetic wave.

v = c/n

Slower inside matter

In a medium, light drives charges that re-radiate slightly out of step. The superposition of the original and re-radiated waves advances more slowly. Individual photons still travel at c between interactions; the wave is what slows.

n = n(ω)

Dispersion

The refractive index depends on frequency, which is why a prism separates colours. Once different frequencies travel at different speeds, a pulse made of many frequencies no longer has a single well-defined speed.

切伦科夫辐射: the blue glow of something moving too fast.

A charged particle polarises the medium it passes through, and each disturbed point radiates a spherical wavelet at speed c/n. If the particle is slower than those wavelets, they never catch up with each other and nothing special happens. If it is faster, the wavelets pile up along a cone and add coherently, exactly like the bow wave of a boat or the sonic boom of an aircraft.

The cone angle is a measurement

Because cos θ = 1/(βn), measuring the opening angle of the cone gives β directly. Combined with a momentum measurement it yields the particle's mass, which is how ring-imaging Cherenkov detectors identify particles. Set βn below 1 and the cone disappears entirely: this sharp threshold is itself used as a velocity filter.

The glow looks blue because the emitted intensity per unit wavelength rises steeply towards short wavelengths. It is not a spectral line, and it is not the water fluorescing. Reactor pools glow for this reason, and kilometre-scale detectors in ice and water use it to reconstruct neutrino directions.

A wave packet has at least three speeds.

Confusing them produces most of the popular claims about beating light. A single infinite sine wave carries no information at all, because it has no beginning and no features. Information requires a change, and a change requires more than one frequency.

Phase velocity

vp = ω / k

  1. The speed of a surface of constant phase, such as one particular crest.
  2. Defined for a single frequency. A pure sine wave is unmodulated and therefore carries no message.
  3. May exceed c with no consequence. For X-rays in glass n is slightly less than 1, so v_p > c; inside a hollow metal waveguide v_p > c as well.

Used for refraction, interference, and phase matching in nonlinear optics.

Group velocity

vg = dω / dk

  1. The speed of the envelope of a packet built from a narrow band of frequencies.
  2. In transparent, weakly dispersive media it equals the speed of energy transport and is the practical speed of a pulse. Fibre-optic timing budgets are group-velocity budgets.
  3. Near a strong absorption line the dispersion becomes anomalous and v_g can exceed c, become infinite, or turn negative. The packet is reshaped, not transported faster.

A smooth packet already contains its own future in its leading tail. Amplifying that tail moves the peak forward without any information overtaking the front.

Front velocity

always exactly c

  1. The speed of the very first, infinitely sharp onset of a signal that was previously exactly zero.
  2. Sommerfeld and Brillouin showed that this front always travels at c in vacuum, regardless of the medium, because an abrupt edge contains arbitrarily high frequencies and no medium can respond to those.
  3. This is the speed that causality actually constrains. It is why no experiment showing v_g > c has ever transmitted information faster than light.

When you read that something moved faster than light, the useful question is: which of these three was measured?

What is actually exceeding what.

Each entry below is a real, reproducible observation. None of them transmits information faster than light in vacuum, and the reason differs in every case.

Phenomenon What exceeds c Why causality is safe
Cherenkov radiation Nothing exceeds c. The particle exceeds c/n, the phase velocity in the medium. The vacuum speed limit is untouched; only the medium's slower wave is overtaken.
X-rays in glass, waveguides Phase velocity A crest is not a marker you can imprint a message on; group velocity stays below c.
Anomalous dispersion, gain-assisted pulses Group velocity, which can even be negative The pulse is reshaped from information already present in its leading edge. The front never advances past c.
Quantum tunnelling delay Apparent traversal speed of the peak The barrier preferentially transmits the early part of the packet, shifting the peak earlier without a superluminal front.
Astrophysical jets Apparent transverse motion on the sky, sometimes many times c A projection effect: material moving near c almost towards us nearly keeps pace with its own light, compressing the arrival times.
Cosmological recession Recession speed of distant galaxies Not motion through space but expansion of the distance between. No local observer measures anything passing them faster than c.
Entangled measurement correlations Nothing measurable Each side alone sees random outcomes. The correlation appears only after the results are compared over an ordinary channel.

Three different mechanisms, one word.

Redshift means the received wavelength is longer than the emitted one. That single symptom has three distinct causes, and they are not interchangeable. Two of them also stretch the apparent pace of events, and one important case does not.

Two wave traces, the lower one stretched, with tick marks showing pulse arrival times.

Stretch factor

Both the wave and the tick marks stretch by the same factor 1 + z. That is the key point: the cadence of events is stretched exactly as much as the wavelength.

Doppler (kinematic) redshift

1 + z = √((1+β)/(1−β))

The source is moving away through space. Each successive crest is emitted from slightly further away, so it has further to travel and arrives later. The relativistic formula also includes time dilation of the moving emitter, which is why a source moving purely across the line of sight is still redshifted.

Events do appear slowed, by the same factor 1 + z. Approaching sources are blueshifted and appear speeded up, which is why binary star spectra shift back and forth.

Gravitational redshift

1 + z = 1/√(1 − 2GM/rc²)

Light climbing out of a gravitational well is received at lower frequency. In general relativity the cleanest statement is that clocks deeper in the well genuinely run slower relative to distant ones, so fewer crests are emitted per second of the distant observer's time.

Events do appear slowed. Confirmed by Pound and Rebka over 22 metres of a tower in 1959, and corrected for continuously by every satellite navigation system.

Cosmological redshift

1 + z = a(tobs) / a(temit)

Not a velocity effect at all. While light is in transit the distances between galaxies grow, and the wave in flight is stretched along with them. The redshift measures how much the universe expanded during the journey, not how fast the source was moving when it emitted.

Events do appear slowed by 1 + z, and this is measured. Type Ia supernovae at z ≈ 1 are observed to rise and fade about twice as slowly as nearby ones, matching the prediction and ruling out models with no expansion.

The case that does not slow

Scattering and dust reddening

Interstellar dust removes blue light more efficiently than red, so a source looks redder. This is reddening, not redshift: spectral lines stay at their original wavelengths and no timing is affected.

Distinguishing the two matters: the historical tired-light proposal tried to explain redshift by photons losing energy en route, and it was ruled out precisely because it predicts no time dilation of distant events.

Where light is used as an instrument.

Everything known about distant matter arrived as electromagnetic radiation, gravitational waves, or neutrinos. Reading it correctly means undoing what the journey did to it.
