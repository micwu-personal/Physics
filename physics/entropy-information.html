Entropy & information

Entropy appears in engines, probability, coding, quantum states, living systems, and black holes. The formulas resemble one another because they all summarize distributions, but their physical referents, units, and operational meanings are not interchangeable.

Thermodynamic entropy is a state function and a balance law.

For reversible heat transfer, dS = δQrev/T defines the entropy change. Real processes add entropy production. The second law constrains the total entropy of an isolated system; it does not demand that every subsystem become less organized at every instant.

dS = δQrev/TA reversible path defines the state-function difference. Heat is path-dependent; entropy change is not.
ΔSsys = Sin - Sout + SgenOpen systems carry entropy with heat and matter. Internal irreversibility contributes Sgen ≥ 0.
ΔSisolated ≥ 0Equality is the reversible ideal. Friction, diffusion, viscous flow, finite-temperature heat transfer, and chemical reaction produce entropy.
Common myth corrected

Local entropy can decrease.

A refrigerator cools its interior, a crystal orders as it grows, and a cell builds structures. Each is an open subsystem. The surroundings gain at least as much entropy as the subsystem loses once energy and matter flows are counted.

Statistical entropy explains why macroscopic irreversibility emerges.

Microscopic equations can be reversible while overwhelmingly many compatible microstates look macroscopically equilibrated. Coarse-graining records the variables we resolve and groups the rest into macrostates; it is part of the physical description, not a trick.

Equal-weight microstates

Boltzmann entropy

W counts microscopic arrangements compatible with the same macrostate. A macrostate with enormously more compatible arrangements is overwhelmingly more likely.

S = kB ln W
Probability distributions

Gibbs entropy

The Gibbs form weights states by probability. Microcanonical, canonical, and grand-canonical ensembles differ by which quantities are fixed and which may fluctuate.

S = -kBΣpi ln pi
Identity matters

Mixing and the Gibbs paradox

Mixing distinguishable gases adds entropy; merely removing a partition between truly identical gases does not add the same term. Quantum indistinguishability makes the counting consistent.

Small systems fluctuate

Fluctuation theorems

Small systems can briefly show entropy-decreasing trajectories. Jarzynski and Crooks relations quantify those rare fluctuations while preserving the statistical content of the second law.

The same distribution can answer a physics question or a coding question.

Adjust the bias. The numerical shape changes in parallel, but the left result is entropy per Boltzmann constant for a physical ensemble while the right result is uncertainty in bits for a message source.

Microstates ↔ messages comparator

A uniform four-symbol source has maximum uncertainty and needs the longest average code. A heavily biased source is more predictable and compressible. The statistical ledger reads the same probabilities as weights over physical states.

Exact calculation
Bars are a probability model. Log base 2 gives bits; natural logarithms paired with kB give thermodynamic units only when the probabilities describe physical states under the required assumptions.

Information theory measures uncertainty, dependence, and reliable communication.

Shannon entropy is not a substance stored in a message. It is a property of a probability model. Coding theorems turn that model into limits on compression and noisy communication.

Source coding

Shannon entropy

H is the minimum asymptotic average description length for an ideal code matched to a source. It is measured in bits for log2 and nats for ln.

H(X) = -Σp(x)log p(x)
Dependence

Mutual and conditional information

Conditional entropy asks what uncertainty remains after another variable is known. Mutual information asks how much knowing one variable reduces uncertainty about the other.

I(X;Y)=H(X)-H(X|Y)
Noisy communication

Channel capacity

Capacity is the greatest rate at which a channel can carry information with arbitrarily small error using sufficiently long, well-designed codes. It is not the raw symbol rate.

C = max I(X;Y)
Comparing models

Relative entropy and data processing

KL divergence measures how distinguishable one distribution is from another; it is asymmetric and not a metric. Local post-processing cannot create new information about the original source.

DKL(P||Q) ≥ 0

Information becomes thermodynamic when a physical operation makes it so.

Information must be represented by physical states, but information is not itself energy or matter. Landauer's principle concerns the heat cost of logically irreversible erasure in a thermal environment—not a universal energy stored inside each bit.

Q ≥ kBT ln 2

Landauer bound

Resetting one unknown bit to a standard value while coupled to a bath dissipates at least kBT ln 2 in the quasistatic ideal. Faster or imperfect hardware usually costs more.

measure → use → reset

Maxwell's demon

A demon can correlate memory with molecules and use feedback, but a reusable cycle must eventually manage and reset memory. Full accounting restores the second law.

physical carrier ≠ information

Information is physical

The phrase means records require physical degrees of freedom and obey physical dynamics. It does not mean information is a fifth form of matter, or that every bit automatically contains kBT ln 2 of energy.

Quantum entropy distinguishes total purity from local uncertainty.

The density matrix describes pure states, classical mixtures, and subsystems of entangled states in one language. A total entangled state can be pure while either subsystem is mixed.

Quantum state entropy

von Neumann entropy

A pure state has zero von Neumann entropy. A mixed state has positive entropy set by the eigenvalues of its density matrix.

S(ρ) = -Tr(ρ log ρ)
Correlations across a cut

Entanglement entropy

Tracing out one part of an entangled pure state leaves a mixed reduced state. Its entropy measures entanglement for a bipartite pure state and often scales with boundary area in many-body ground states.

Open quantum dynamics

Decoherence and channels

Entanglement with an environment suppresses accessible interference and creates classical-looking records. A quantum channel describes noise, loss, environment, or measurement; decoherence alone is not a complete interpretation of measurement.

Information constraints

No-cloning and error correction

An unknown quantum state cannot be copied perfectly. Quantum error correction instead spreads logical information nonlocally across many physical qubits so local errors can be diagnosed without reading the encoded state directly.

A black hole turns geometry into thermodynamics.

Classical horizon area never decreases in ordinary processes. Quantum field theory on curved spacetime assigns the hole a temperature and entropy. The result is among the deepest clues that spacetime and information share microscopic structure.

SBH = kBA/(4ℓP²)Bekenstein-Hawking entropy scales with horizon area, not interior volume. For astrophysical holes it is enormous.
TH = ℏc³/(8πGMkB)Larger black holes are colder. Hawking radiation is a semiclassical prediction and has not been directly observed from an astrophysical black hole.
Δ(Soutside+SBH) ≥ 0The generalized second law combines ordinary exterior entropy with black-hole entropy.
Semiclassical theory

The information paradox

Hawking's calculation makes the outgoing radiation look thermal. Complete evaporation would then seem to erase distinctions among initial quantum states, conflicting with unitary quantum evolution.

Theoretical benchmark

The Page curve

If evaporation is unitary, radiation entropy should rise, turn over near the Page time, and eventually fall. The curve is not an astronomical measurement.

Quantum-gravity approaches

Complementarity, firewalls, islands

These frameworks reorganize where information is encoded and which semiclassical regions count in the entropy. They are active theory, not observed structures near a horizon.

Motivating principle

Area laws and holography

Horizon entropy and entanglement area laws helped motivate the holographic principle: a quantum-gravity description of a region may encode information according to its boundary. This is not the claim that our everyday world is a literal projection screen.

Life exports entropy; gravity changes what high entropy looks like.

These are the places where the word 'disorder' fails most dramatically. A cell can become organized while dissipating free energy, and gravitating matter can clump while the total accessible entropy rises.

Chemistry

Free energy, mixing, reaction affinity

Reaction direction at fixed temperature and pressure follows Gibbs free energy, not entropy alone. Enthalpy and entropy compete, while catalysts alter rates without changing the equilibrium free-energy difference.

Molecular biology

Open, driven, nonequilibrium systems

Cells maintain gradients, copy molecular information, fold proteins, and build membranes by consuming chemical free energy and releasing heat and waste. Life does not evade the second law.

Soft and active matter

Entropic elasticity and self-assembly

Stretching a polymer removes possible conformations, producing an entropic restoring force. Crowding, depletion, and solvent entropy can drive organized structures even when energy alone does not favor them.

The universe

Low-entropy beginning, structure, horizons

The early universe was hot and smooth yet gravitationally low-entropy relative to later possibilities. Structure formation and black-hole growth raise gravitational entropy. No universally accepted local gravitational entropy density exists outside special settings.

Interpretive frontier

There may be more than one useful arrow of time.

Thermodynamic irreversibility, radiation, records, cosmological expansion, and psychological memory are connected but not simply identical. The low-entropy boundary condition of the early universe remains central to explaining why macroscopic time has a preferred direction.
