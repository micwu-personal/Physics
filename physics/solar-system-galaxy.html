Rocky planetsMercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars orbit inside the giant planets. Their ordering is observed; the diagram enlarges every body.
Main belt and near-Earth asteroidsCeres and Vesta represent a broad belt between Mars and Jupiter; Bennu represents a near-Earth population whose members cross or approach Earth's orbit.
Giant-planet regionJupiter and Saturn are gas giants; Uranus and Neptune are ice giants. Resonances scatter and trap smaller bodies, including Centaurs between the giant planets.

The Sun has no single edge.

Solar-wind pressure, magnetic structure, bound small bodies, and gravitational dominance describe different physical questions. Their boundaries must not be collapsed into one circle.

A gravity assist rotates a velocity vector before it changes a heliocentric speed.

In the planet's frame, an ideal two-body flyby turns the incoming velocity while keeping its far-away magnitude nearly unchanged. Add the moving planet's velocity again and the Sun-frame speed and direction can change.

The Milky Way is a barred disk with fuzzy boundaries and a long assembly history.

Starlight maps a thin and thick disk, central bar and bulge, spiral structure, stellar halo, and satellite systems. Gas and dust reveal other components; motions imply much more gravitating mass than luminous matter supplies.

Claim linksShen & Zheng 2020ESA anatomyEilers et al. 2019

Orientation: This face-on map follows the Shen & Zheng (2020) figure convention: rotation is clockwise on this screen. Mirroring the map or viewing the disk from the opposite side reverses the apparent handedness; the animation moves tracers, not a rigid pinwheel.

Morphology modelThe far side of our Galaxy is obscured and spiral-arm maps combine many tracers. Disk diameter and thickness depend on which stellar population and density threshold define the edge.

Shape and thicknessA useful round number for the luminous disk is about 100,000 light-years across. ESA's Gaia anatomy gives order-of-magnitude heights near 700 light-years for the thin disk and 3,000 for the thick disk; populations and density thresholds change any quoted edge.
Rotation and inferred dark matterOrbital speeds remain high well beyond most visible mass. Together with lensing, satellite motion, and cosmological evidence, this supports an extended gravitating component called dark matter; the diagram does not claim that component has been directly imaged.
Interactions, not isolationThe Sagittarius dwarf and Magellanic Clouds interact with the Milky Way, leaving streams and perturbations. The Milky Way and Andromeda are approaching, but the exact timing and geometry of a future close passage remain model-dependent; a 2025 analysis including Local Group uncertainties found roughly even odds, not certainty, of merger within 10 billion years.

The Milky Way assembled; it did not appear fully formed.

Scrub cosmic time through early fragments, major accretion, disk growth, the present Local Group, and a schematic future approach with Andromeda. Early stages are reconstructions from stellar ages, chemistry, motions, simulations, and high-redshift analogues.
